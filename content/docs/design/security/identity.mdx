Before anything in Arkitekt can read or write data, it has to prove who it is.
Arkitekt handles this with well-established standards rather than anything
bespoke, OAuth2, OpenID Connect and signed JWT tokens, so that the same identity
works consistently across every service.

## Lok, the coordinator

[**Lok**](/docs/design/services/lok) is the service responsible for authentication
and authorization. Conceptually it is the **coordinator**: arguably the single
most important part of the platform, because everything else trusts the identities
it issues.

The coordinator's job is two things that go hand in hand: **auth** (proving who you
and your apps are) and **discovery** (helping apps and servers find and trust each
other). Lok handles the auth; the discovery side shows up as the [Fakts
protocol](/docs/design/architecture/service-discovery) on a local network, and as
the [mesh](/docs/design/security/mesh) across networks, where the same coordinator
role is run as a *coordination server*. It is one control plane wearing two hats,
not two unrelated systems.

Lok issues **JWT tokens**, cryptographically signed claims about a user and the
application acting on their behalf. Because the signature can be verified by any
service on its own, there is **no central session store** to consult on every
request: each service independently checks the token it is handed. This is what
lets Arkitekt's services stay independent and scale without a shared bottleneck.

## Two-step authentication: users *and* apps

Logging in to Arkitekt authenticates **two** parties at once: **you**, and the
**app** acting for you.

The first time a new app connects, you are asked to approve the connection and the
permissions it requests, exactly like pressing "Log in with Google" on a website.
This double-authentication is central to how Arkitekt keeps you in control: the
app never impersonates you, it acts *as itself, on your authority*. You can read
more in [Apps](/docs/design/terminology/app).

## Fakts: discovery and app authorization

Apps also need to *find* a deployment and get their configuration. That is the job
of the **Fakts** protocol, described in [Service
Discovery](/docs/design/architecture/service-discovery).

Fakts uses a device-code-style flow, similar in spirit to OAuth2:

1. The app presents its **Manifest** (identifier, version, requested scopes).
2. It receives a user-validation code.
3. You approve the app in the browser.
4. The app receives a token and fetches the deployment configuration it needs.

On the client side, two small libraries pair up to do this: **Fakts** retrieves
configuration, and **Herre** is the asynchronous OAuth2 client that manages the
tokens. (Note that *Herre* is a client library, not a server, the server is Lok.)

## Scopes

An app declares the access rights it needs as **scopes** in its
[Manifest](/docs/design/terminology/manifest), for example `openid` and `email`.
Scopes are how you reason about "what can this app actually do with my data" before
you approve it.

<Callout type="warn" title="Scopes are still being enforced">
Scope-based access control is **not yet fully enforced** by the platform. Enforcing
it too strictly today would hinder the development of new apps, so we are
introducing it gradually. Treat scopes as the intended model, not yet a hard
guarantee.
</Callout>

## Social login

You can sign in with an external provider such as **GitHub, Google or ORCID**. When
you do, the provider hands Arkitekt a minimal identity token, typically just your
email address and name, purely so we can create or match your login. Arkitekt does
**not** gain access to your provider account. See the [Privacy
Policy](/docs/privacy) for exactly what is exchanged.
