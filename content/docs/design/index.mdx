At its heart, Arkitekt is a **hub**. Picture a hub-and-spoke: a single center with
everything else radiating out from it. Arkitekt is that center. Your **apps**, the
tools that do the actual work, are the spokes. They do not talk to each other
directly; they connect *to* the hub, and everything, data, compute, results,
flows *through* it. That is what makes a loose scattering of tools behave like one
coherent platform.

The hub plays two roles at once:

- It is a **middleman**, brokering requests between apps so they can rely on one
  another without knowing anything about each other.
- It is a **datahub**, a central place where your lab's data lives and can be
  explored, in real time, throughout its lifecycle.

What the hub does *not* do is decide who is allowed in, or introduce apps to it in
the first place. That is a separate job, and it belongs to the **coordinator**
(described below). Keeping the two apart matters: the hub holds your data and does
the brokering, while the coordinator handles identity and discovery.

The rest of these docs unpack the pieces of this picture. The three you meet first
are:

- The **Hub** itself (and the services it is built from)
- **Apps** (the spokes that connect to it)
- The **Coordinator** (a separate piece: authentication and discovery)

## The Hub

The hub is the center of everything: the part you install and run, and the point
every app connects to. It is a **middleman** and a **datahub** in one, it brokers
communication between apps, and it stores the data they produce so the whole lab
can build on it. On its own the hub does very little "analysis"; its job is to
broker and to remember. The heavy lifting is left to the apps, and identity is left
to the coordinator.

The hub is modular. It is built from a set of [**Services**](/docs/design/services)
(like Mikro for data, or Rekuest for tasks), and when you deploy Arkitekt you choose
[which services](/docs/design/deployment/) your hub needs.

<Callout type="info" title="Hub vs. Server">

You will also see us call the hub a **Server** (or a **Deployment**). That is the
*technical* name for what the hub runs on. A `Server` is a logical entity, not
necessarily one physical machine: it can be a single computer under your desk, a
cluster, or an adapting set of virtual machines in the cloud. Whichever it is, the
*concept* it realizes is the hub, the central thing your apps connect to.

</Callout>

## Apps

Apps are the **spokes**, and the true stars of any work done on Arkitekt. While the
hub only connects and remembers, apps are the entities that actually *do* something
with your data, from a web app that visualizes results to a machine-learning model
running in a container.

Crucially, apps connect only to the hub, never directly to one another. An app
exposes part of its functionality to the hub, and the hub makes that functionality
available to *other* apps, to you, and to workflows. This is how a net of
capabilities grows across your lab without any two tools ever needing to know about
each other. To read more, see [Apps vs Services](/docs/design/terminology/apps-vs-services).

## The Coordinator

The coordinator is a piece of its own, separate from the hub. It sits between you,
your apps, and the hub, and its job is to make sure everyone is who they say they
are and can find one another. On the surface it is the most boring part of the
platform, and under the hood probably the single most important. It is the
**control plane**: the part that manages who can access which services and apps, and
how the pieces of a deployment find and trust each other. In Arkitekt this role is
filled by [**Lok**](/docs/design/services/lok), and it covers two jobs that belong
together:

- **Authentication and authorization** of users and apps, so every request is made
  by someone who is logged in and allowed to make it.
- **Discovery**, so apps and servers can locate one another, both on a local network
  via the [Fakts protocol](/docs/design/architecture/service-discovery), and across
  networks via the [mesh](/docs/design/security/mesh), where the same coordinator
  role runs as a *coordination server*.

Without the coordinator, you and your apps could never safely reach the hub:
nothing would know who anyone is, and the apps and the hub would never meet. For the
full picture, see [Identity & Access](/docs/design/security/identity).
