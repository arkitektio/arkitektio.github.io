Before you install anything, five minutes on the three pieces you are about to set up. Every later
page builds on them.

<HubSharing coordinator />

You put an image into the hub through an app, and a colleague opens the same image from somewhere
else, in the app they like. Above it all, the coordinator knows who each of you is, and never holds
the data. The rest of this page takes the three pieces one at a time.

## The hub: where your data lives

The **hub** is a server, on a machine in your lab or on your own computer. Everything else in
Arkitekt connects to it. It has two jobs:

- **It stores your data.** Images, masks, tables, and the record of how each one was made, in one
  place instead of in folders spread over several computers.
- **It passes work between your tools.** When you ask for an image to be segmented, the hub finds a
  tool that can do it and hands the work over.

The hub does no analysis of its own. It brokers and it remembers.

## Apps: the tools that do the work

An **app** is any program that connects to a hub: Orkestrator, the desktop app you click around
in; napari or Fiji; a Python script; a segmentation model in a container. When an app connects,
it tells the hub what it can do. Each of those things is an **action**, like "Otsu Thresholding:
Image → Mask".

Apps only ever talk to the hub, never to each other. So a viewer can ask for a segmentation without
knowing which app does it or on which machine it runs, and you can swap one app for another without
the rest noticing.

## The coordinator: who is who, and where

The **coordinator** is the third piece, and the least visible one. It doesn't store your data
and it doesn't run your analyses. It answers two questions for everyone else:

- **Who are you?** It holds the accounts: people, the organizations they belong to, and the apps
  acting for them.
- **Where is the hub?** It knows which hubs belong to your organization, and introduces your apps
  to them.

Once it has introduced an app to a hub, it steps aside: data goes directly between the two.
[go.arkitekt.live](https://go.arkitekt.live) is a coordinator we run for everyone, and both paths
start with an account on it.

## Why a coordinator at all?

It is fair to ask why apps can't just connect to the hub directly. Three reasons:

1. **Hubs are hard to find.** A hub in a lab usually sits behind a firewall, on a network that
   changes addresses and drops incoming connections. You can rarely "just point an app at a
   server". The coordinator knows where your hubs are, and can connect machines across networks
   through the [mesh](/docs/run/security/mesh) when they are not on the same one.
2. **One account, many hubs.** Without a coordinator every hub would keep its own users and
   passwords, and you would have one login per hub: one for the facility, one for your lab, one on
   your laptop. With it, you sign in once, and every hub of your organization knows who you are.
3. **Apps act for you, and should say so.** When Basiks thresholds an image, it does that on your
   behalf. The coordinator gives each app its own identity and only the permissions you approved.
   That way the hub can record that the mask was made *by Basiks, on your behalf*, and not just
   "by you".

Keeping the coordinator separate from the hub is deliberate, too. The hub holds your data and knows
nothing about passwords. The coordinator holds logins and never sees your data. Neither can leak what
it doesn't have.

<Callout type="info" title="Does it have to be ours?">

No. go.arkitekt.live is the easiest coordinator to start with, but you can use your institute's own
or [run your own](/docs/run/topology#three-ways-to-meet), and work fully offline.

</Callout>

| Piece | What it does | Where it runs |
|---|---|---|
| **Hub** | Stores your data, passes work between apps | Your lab, or your computer |
| **Apps** | Do the actual work, and announce what they can do as actions | Anywhere: your laptop, a GPU server, the hub's machine |
| **Coordinator** | Knows who is who, and introduces apps to hubs | go.arkitekt.live, or your own |

<Quiz>
  <Question q="Which piece stores your images?">
    The hub. Apps work on them, and the coordinator never sees them.
  </Question>
  <Question q="Do two apps ever talk to each other directly?">
    No. They only talk to the hub, which passes work between them.
  </Question>
  <Question q="Name one reason Arkitekt needs a coordinator.">
    Any of: apps need help finding a hub behind firewalls and changing networks; one account should
    work on every hub of your organization; apps act for you and need their own identity and the
    permissions you gave them.
