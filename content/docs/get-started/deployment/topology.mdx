The work in Arkitekt is done by apps, and apps can run anywhere: on a GPU box under your desk, a
workstation down the hall, a node in the cloud. They still need a central server, the
[hub](/docs/concepts#the-hub), to register with, to find one another through, and to keep track of
data and results. Before any of that, the apps and the hub have to **find each other**.

Twenty years ago that was easy: give the server a public address and point the apps at it. Today
servers in labs sit behind NAT and institutional firewalls, on networks that change addresses and
drop inbound connections. Part of Arkitekt's deployment design exists only to solve this meeting
problem.

## Three ways to meet [#three-ways-to-meet]

The three options differ in **who runs what**. Each is a tradeoff, not a difficulty level.

**Use a Kommunity partner.** An institution that hosts an Arkitekt server and opens it to others
runs the whole platform, and you bring only your apps. You run no hardware, no Docker and no
updates. In exchange your data and compute live on infrastructure you don't own, so you have to trust
the partner with both. This option is **demo-only for now**: good for a first look, not for sensitive
or production work. Your local imaging facility is the kind of place that might run, or be willing
to run, a partner server.

**Run your own hub and use our coordination server.** You run the hub on a machine in your lab, so
your data and compute stay under your control. Your hub and apps register with the
community-operated **coordination server**, which introduces them and helps them find each other
across NATs and networks. After that, traffic flows directly between them, and the coordination
server never sees your data (see [The Arkitekt Mesh](#arkitekt-mesh)). This is the **recommended
start for most labs**. The tradeoff is a dependency you don't control: while the coordination server
is down, no new connections can be brokered, and you trust a third party to run authentication and
discovery, though never the data.

**Self-host everything, including the coordination server.** You run the hub and your own
coordination server, so nothing depends on anyone outside your walls. This is the most work: you
operate, update and secure the coordinator, and reachability across networks becomes your problem.
In return you can run fully **offline or air-gapped**. It also suits **tear-up / teardown
testing**: standing up a complete platform with its coordinator, running against it and tearing it
down again, for CI, reproducible demos or a server that resets on a schedule.

<Callout type="info" title="Two kinds of 'server'">

The **central server** is the hub: it holds your data and runs the services. The **coordination
server** is the [coordinator](/docs/concepts#the-coordinator): it only handles authentication and
discovery so that apps and hubs can meet, and never touches your data. The three options are three
answers to who runs each of them.

</Callout>

Where the apps run is the same in every column: anywhere. What changes is who runs the servers, and
with it where your data lives and what you depend on.

| | Kommunity partner | Your hub + our coordination server | Fully self-hosted |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Who runs the central server** | The partner | You, in your lab | You |
| **Who runs the coordination server** | The partner | Arkitekt community | You |
| **Where your data lives** | Partner's infrastructure | Your lab | Your lab |
| **Setup and ops effort** | None | Low to moderate (hub only) | High (hub and coordinator) |
| **External dependency** | Total, on the partner | Discovery and auth only | None |
| **Works offline / air-gapped** | No | No | Yes |
| **Data control** | Lowest: you trust the partner | High: coordinator sees no data | Highest: nothing leaves your walls |
| **Readiness** | Demo-only for now | Recommended for most labs | Production / advanced |
| **Best for** | A first look without setup | Most labs that want data kept local | Air-gapped sites, CI, tear-up / teardown testing |

The installation itself is the same in every case; see
[Install your first hub](/docs/get-started/deployment/first-hub).

## The Arkitekt Mesh [#arkitekt-mesh]

On one trusted local network, apps reach the hub directly. From anywhere else (your laptop on the
train, a collaborator at another institute, a GPU node behind a different NAT) the **mesh** connects
them without opening firewall ports. It is a private network per organization in the style of
Tailscale: the coordinator handles authentication and discovery, and your images, tables and task
data flow directly between the endpoints. How it works, and why it is the secure default, is
explained in [The Mesh](/docs/concepts/security/mesh). The mesh is **optional and experimental**.

### How a hub joins [#joining-the-mesh]

You decide when you create a hub with Konstruktor: stay off the mesh, join your organization's mesh
through the community coordination server, or bring a key from a coordinator you run yourself.
Joining is not the same as being *advertised*. Once the hub has its mesh address, add it to the
hub's advertised addresses and authorize the hub again, so that apps coming in over the mesh are
told where to find it. The options and CLI flags are in
[Join the mesh](/docs/get-started/deployment/running#join-the-mesh).

<Callout type="warn" title="The mesh is young">

The mesh is evolving quickly, and its options may change between releases. Stay on a recent version.
If you have trouble joining or reaching endpoints, see
[Troubleshooting](/docs/get-started/deployment/troubleshooting) or open an issue.

</Callout>
