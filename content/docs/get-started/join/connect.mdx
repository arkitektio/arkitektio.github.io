You are in their organization. Now you connect **Orkestrator**, the desktop app you use Arkitekt
with, to their hub.

## Do it

Download Orkestrator and open it. It needs no admin rights:

<OrkestratorGrid />

1. Click **Sign in with go.arkitekt.live**. Your browser opens.
2. Sign in with your account.
3. On the approval page, pick their hub under **Assign to hub**. If you are in one organization
   with one hub, it is already selected.
4. Leave **Join the organization's mesh** switched on and click **Allow access**.

Back in Orkestrator, you are connected, and you see what is already on the hub.

## What just happened

Orkestrator is an **app**, and signing in went through the same three parties every app goes
through:

1. **Orkestrator asked the coordinator.** It opened go.arkitekt.live in your browser, so you could
   sign in there. Orkestrator never sees your password.
2. **You decided what it may do.** The approval page showed which hub it wants to use, and on
   whose behalf. **Allow access** gave Orkestrator permission to act for you, on that hub.
3. **The coordinator introduced them.** Orkestrator learned where the hub is, and received a token
   the hub checks on every request.

You never typed an address. Your account knows the hubs of your organization.

## Reaching a hub on another network: the mesh

When you run your own hub, it usually sits on the same network as you. Somebody else's hub often
doesn't: it is in the lab and you are at home, or it is at the facility and you are in your office
across campus, behind a different firewall. That is what the switch you left on is for.

The **mesh** is a private, encrypted network between the machines of your organization:

- The coordinator hands Orkestrator a **key** for your organization's mesh. The hub has one too.
- With those keys, the two open an encrypted **WireGuard** tunnel directly to each other. No ports
  to open, no VPN to set up.
- Your images and results travel through that tunnel, **between your machine and the hub**. The
  coordinator knows *that* the two talk, not *what* they say.

If the hub isn't on a mesh, the switch simply does nothing, and you reach the hub on the network you
share with it. More in [The Mesh](/docs/run/security/mesh).
