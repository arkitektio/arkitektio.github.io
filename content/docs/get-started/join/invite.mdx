You want to use a hub that somebody else runs. Before Orkestrator can show you anything on it, you
have to be let into the **organization** that owns it. That is what the invite is for.

## Do it

<Tabs items={["Somebody sent me an invite", "I have no invite yet"]}>
<Tab value="Somebody sent me an invite">

1. **Open the link.** It looks like `https://go.arkitekt.live/invite/…`.
2. **Sign up or log in.** No account yet? Press **Sign Up** on the invite page, with your email
   address, Google, ORCID or your institution's login. You come back to the invite afterwards.
3. **Accept.**

Signed in with a different account than the one invited? The invite page tells you so: sign out
and sign back in with the right one.

</Tab>
<Tab value="I have no invite yet">

Ask whoever set up Arkitekt where you work: your lab's admin, your imaging facility, or a
collaborator who wants to share data with you. On [go.arkitekt.live](https://go.arkitekt.live) they
open their organization, go to **Invites**, create a link and send it to you.

Nobody to ask? Then [bring your own hub](/docs/get-started/own-hub/super-quick) instead.

</Tab>
</Tabs>

## What an organization is

Everything in Arkitekt happens inside an **organization**: the group your data belongs to, such as
a lab, a facility or a project. Hubs belong to organizations, and so does everything stored on them.

Two rules make it work:

- **It is a hard boundary.** Data never crosses from one organization to another. A facility can
  run one hub for ten groups, and each group stays in its own world.
- **Inside, it is open by default.** Members can see and build on each other's data, because
  science is collaborative. Admins can restrict that where they need to.

More in [Organization](/docs/concepts/terminology#organization).

## What the invite changed

Accepting did not create anything on the hub. It changed one record on the **coordinator**
(go.arkitekt.live, see [the basics](/docs/get-started/basics)): you are now a member of that
organization. Every hub of the organization trusts the coordinator, so every one of them now
accepts you. You never get a separate account on the hub itself.

Not every invite makes you a full member. If someone only wanted to share part of their work with
you, you join as a **guest**: you see what they shared, not the whole organization.

<Callout type="info" title="What the coordinator knows about you">

Your email address, your name if you gave one, and which organizations you are in. Not your data:
that lives on their hub and never passes through go.arkitekt.live. Details in the
[privacy policy](/docs/privacy#tier-2-an-account-on-goarkitektlive). If your invite link pointed
somewhere other than go.arkitekt.live, that is the coordinator their hub trusts instead; everything
else is the same.

</Callout>

<Quiz>
  <Question q="What does accepting an invite give you?">
    Membership in an organization, and with it access to every hub that organization owns.
  </Question>
  <Question q="Does accepting create an account on the hub?">
    No. Your membership is recorded on the coordinator, and the hub trusts the coordinator.
  </Question>
  <Question q="What is the difference between a member and a guest?">
    A member sees the organization's data (open by default). A guest only sees what was shared with
    them.
  </Question>
</Quiz>
