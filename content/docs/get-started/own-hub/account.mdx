Arkitekt starts with an account, before you install anything. This page is about what that account
is (and is not) used for.

<Callout type="info" title="New to Arkitekt?">

Five minutes on [Understanding the basics](/docs/concepts/basics) first make the next pages easier:
what a hub, an app and the coordinator are.

</Callout>

## Do it

Open the [sign-up page on go.arkitekt.live](https://go.arkitekt.live/account/signup) and sign up,
with your email address, Google, ORCID or your institution's login. That's all for this step.

<Callout type="info" title="You can go completely without us">

Arkitekt does not need go.arkitekt.live. You can point it at your institute's own coordination
server, or [self-host everything](/docs/get-started/deployment/topology#three-ways-to-meet), coordinator included,
and run fully offline. For your first experience, use go.arkitekt.live anyway: there is nothing to
set up, and you can move away from it later. Before you sign up, you may want to read what we
store ([privacy policy](/docs/privacy#tier-2-an-account-on-goarkitektlive)) and how the
[mesh](/docs/concepts/security/mesh) connects machines without us seeing what they send.

</Callout>

## What go.arkitekt.live is

go.arkitekt.live is the **coordinator** from [the basics](/docs/concepts/basics) that we run for
everyone: it holds your account and your organization, and introduces your apps to your hub. Then
it gets out of the way. Your images, results and tasks go **directly** between your apps and your
hub, never through it.

## What it stores, and what it never sees

| go.arkitekt.live knows | go.arkitekt.live never sees |
|---|---|
| Your email address, and your name if you give one | Your images, tables and results |
| Your organization and who is in it | The tasks you run and their inputs |
| Which hubs and apps belong to it | What your apps and hub say to each other |

This split is deliberate. Your data lives on hardware you control, and the coordinator only does
the "meeting". If you later connect machines across networks (a laptop at home, a GPU in another
building), the coordinator also hands out keys for the [mesh](/docs/concepts/security/mesh): an
encrypted network between *your* machines, where it still only knows *that* two machines talk, not
*what* they say. This quickstart keeps everything on one network and does not use the mesh.

More on the split between hub and coordinator in [Design](/docs/concepts#the-coordinator) and
[Identity & Access](/docs/concepts/security/identity).

<Quiz>
  <Question q="What does go.arkitekt.live store about you?">
    Your email address, your name if you give one, your organization and which hubs and apps belong
    to it. Nothing of your data.
  </Question>
  <Question q="Where do your images go when an app works on them?">
    Directly between the app and your hub. They never pass through go.arkitekt.live.
  </Question>
  <Question q="Do you have to use go.arkitekt.live?">
    No. You can use your institute's own coordination server, or run your own and work fully
    offline. go.arkitekt.live is simply the easiest place to start.
  </Question>
</Quiz>
