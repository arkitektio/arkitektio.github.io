The hub is the center of Arkitekt: everything else connects to it. On this page you start one on
your own computer with a single command.

## Do it

You need **Docker** installed and running
([Docker Desktop](https://www.docker.com/products/docker-desktop/) on macOS and Windows), and
ideally 16 GB of RAM (see [Requirements](/docs/get-started/deployment/requirements)). Then run this in a terminal:

```bash tab="macOS / Linux"
curl -fsSL https://raw.githubusercontent.com/arkitektio/konstruktor/main/install.sh | sh
```

```powershell tab="Windows"
irm https://raw.githubusercontent.com/arkitektio/konstruktor/main/install.ps1 | iex
```

This installs **Konstruktor**, the tool that sets up and runs hubs, and asks a few questions.
Accept the defaults with <kbd>Enter</kbd>: yes, put it on your `PATH`, and yes, create a hub now.
When it asks for a name, give the hub a short one, for example `my-hub`.

Konstruktor then shows a **link and a code**. Open the link, sign in with the account you just
created, and accept the hub. The hub then starts. The first start downloads the images and takes a
few minutes.

## What a hub is

As [the basics](/docs/concepts/basics) put it: the hub stores your data and passes work between
your tools, and does no analysis of its own. The actual work is done by **apps**, which you install
on page 4. Here is what that looks like on your machine.

## What runs inside it

A hub is not one program but a small set of **services**, each with one job, behind a single
gateway. The ones you will meet in this quickstart:

| Service | Its job |
|---|---|
| **Mikro** | Stores images and their metadata |
| **Rekuest** | Knows which actions your apps offer, and passes tasks to them |
| **Kabinet** | The App Store: installs apps and runs them |

The default hub also starts a **plugin engine**: the part that runs the apps you install from the
App Store, as containers on this machine.

Underneath, all of this is a plain Docker Compose project in a folder. Konstruktor is a convenience
for creating, updating and backing it up, not a black box. See
[What you are setting up](/docs/get-started/deployment/what-you-are-setting-up).

## Why you had to accept it

A hub keeps **no accounts of its own**. Your account and your organization live on the coordinator
(page 1), and your data lives on the hub. Accepting the hub tells the coordinator: *this hub belongs
to my organization, and the people and apps in it may use it*. From then on, when you sign in
somewhere, the coordinator can vouch for you to the hub, and the hub trusts it.

That is also why you never create a user account on the hub itself: people and apps always sign in
through the coordinator.
