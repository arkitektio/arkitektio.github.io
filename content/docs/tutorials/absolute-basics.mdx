## One place for your data: the hub

Arkitekt is built around a central data storage. We call it the **hub**.

The hub is a program that runs on one computer: a server in your lab or facility, or your own
workstation. Everything else in Arkitekt connects to it. It does two things for you:

1. **It stores your data.** Your images, the results of your analyses and the notes you make about
   them live in the hub, in one place, instead of in folders spread over several computers.
2. **It lets you look at your data.** Because everything is in one place, you can browse it, open
   an image and inspect it from any computer that can reach the hub.

That is the whole idea. The rest of Arkitekt builds on it.

## What happens to an image

Follow one image through the hub:

1. **You put it in.** You drag a file from your computer into Arkitekt. The hub now keeps it, and
   remembers who uploaded it and which dataset it belongs to.
2. **You look at it.** You open the image in Orkestrator, the Arkitekt desktop app, and inspect it
   there. If you prefer napari or Fiji, they open the same image
   straight from the hub. You do not download or copy anything.
3. **Tools work on it.** An analysis tool takes the image from the hub, does its work and puts the
   result back. The result sits next to the original, and the hub remembers how it was made.

<Callout type="info" title="The hub does not analyse anything">

The hub stores and shows your data and passes work around. The analysis itself is done by **apps**
that connect to it, such as napari, Fiji or a script somebody wrote. You will meet them in the next
tutorials.

</Callout>

## From anywhere, and together

<HubSharing />

Because the image lives in the hub and not on one laptop, you reach it from any computer that can
reach the hub: at the microscope, in your office, at home. You upload it once, on the left of the
picture, and open it wherever you are.

So can other people. Arkitekt is a **collaborative platform**: to show an image to a colleague you
do not send them a copy, you give them access to it. They open the very same image, from their own
computer, in the app they like.

## Who gets in, and why you need an account

Sharing only works if **you decide who gets access to your data**. And to let somebody in, or keep
somebody out, the hub first has to know *who they are*, and who you are.

Something has to answer that question: an account. In Arkitekt, whoever hosts your hub can provide
the accounts, for example your institute. To make things easy we provide them too, on
[go.arkitekt.live](https://go.arkitekt.live). That is why the next tutorial asks you to create an
account.

<Callout type="warn" title="Your data is never stored with us">

The account holds only what a login needs: your e-mail address, and your name if you give one (see
the [privacy policy](/docs/privacy#tier-2-an-account-on-goarkitektlive)). Your images, results and
notes stay on your hub, on the computer of whoever runs it. They do not pass through us.

We do one more thing for you: we make your hub easier to find on the internet. When you sign in from
another network, your app learns from your account where your hub is. From then on the app and the
hub talk to each other directly.

The one exception is `demo.arkitekt.live`, a public playground hub that we host so you can try
Arkitekt without installing it. Whatever you upload there is on our server, so treat it as public.

</Callout>

## What you need to continue

A running hub. Either somebody runs one for you and you [join it](/docs/guides/joining), or you
[install your own](/docs/tutorials/first-hub).

## Where to go from here

Each of these tutorials stands on its own, and together they take you from an empty hub to your
first analysis:

1. [Connect with Orkestrator](/docs/tutorials/connect-orkestrator): sign in to a hub and find your way around.
2. [Upload your first images](/docs/tutorials/upload-data): put data into the hub and organise it.
3. [Install your first tool](/docs/tutorials/install-a-tool): add an app that can do something with your data.
4. [Run your first task](/docs/tutorials/run-a-task): let that app work on an image and look at the result.
5. [Open your data in napari and Fiji](/docs/tutorials/napari-and-fiji): view the same image in the tools you already know.
6. [Why so many steps?](/docs/tutorials/why-so-many-steps): a short read on why Arkitekt is split into small apps.
7. [Design your first workflow](/docs/tutorials/design-a-workflow): chain several steps into one analysis.
8. [Run your first workflow](/docs/tutorials/run-a-workflow): run it and see what came out.
