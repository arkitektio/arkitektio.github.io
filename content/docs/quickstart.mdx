By the end of this page you will have started a hub on your own computer, connected to it,
installed an app and run it on an image. It takes about fifteen minutes, most of which is waiting
for downloads. There are no choices to make: everything here uses the defaults.

<Prerequisites>

- A computer running macOS, Linux or Windows, with **Docker** installed and running
  ([Docker Desktop](https://www.docker.com/products/docker-desktop/) on macOS and Windows)
- A reasonably recent machine: 16 GB of RAM is recommended (see [Requirements](/docs/run/requirements))
- An email address, for a free account on go.arkitekt.live (you create it on the way)

</Prerequisites>

<Steps>
<Step>

## Start a hub

The **hub** is the server in the middle: it stores your images and passes work between your tools.
Start one with a single command:

```bash tab="macOS / Linux"
curl -fsSL https://raw.githubusercontent.com/arkitektio/konstruktor/main/install.sh | sh
```

```powershell tab="Windows"
irm https://raw.githubusercontent.com/arkitektio/konstruktor/main/install.ps1 | iex
```

This installs **Konstruktor**, the tool that sets up and runs hubs, and asks a few questions.
Accept the defaults with <kbd>Enter</kbd>: yes, put it on your `PATH`, and yes, create a hub now.
When it asks for a name, give the hub a short one, for example `my-hub`. The hub comes with
everything this quickstart needs, including a **plugin engine** that runs the apps you install.

Konstruktor then shows a **link and a code**. Open the link, sign in to go.arkitekt.live (or create
an account), and accept the hub. A hub keeps no accounts of its own: your account and your
organization live on go.arkitekt.live, and this step connects the new hub to them.

Once accepted, the hub starts. The first start downloads the images and takes a few minutes.

</Step>
<Step>

## Open Orkestrator and sign in

**Orkestrator** is the desktop app you use Arkitekt with. Download it and open it:

<OrkestratorGrid />

1. Click **Sign in with go.arkitekt.live**. Your browser opens.
2. Sign in with the account from step 1.
3. On the approval page, the hub you just started is already selected under **Assign to hub**.
   Click **Allow access**.

Back in Orkestrator, you are connected to your hub. It is empty, for now.

</Step>
<Step>

## Install your first app

Apps do the actual work. They come from the **App Store**, and run on your hub's plugin engine. Install **Basiks**, the standard library: small, general
actions on images.

Click this badge. It opens Orkestrator on the install page for Basiks:

<a href="https://arkitekt.live/deeplink?orkestrator=%2Fdo%2Fv1%2Frepo.install%3Fsource%3Doci%253Aghcr.io%252Fjhnnsrs%252Fbasiks" style={{ display: "inline-block" }}>
  <img src="/img/badge/open-in-arkitekt.svg" alt="Open in Arkitekt: install Basiks" style={{ height: "28px", width: "auto", margin: 0 }} />
</a>

1. Press **Import**. You land on the app's page, with the actions it brings.
2. Press **Install** in the header (it names the version), look over what it asks for and confirm
   with **Authorize**.
3. The dialog asks where to deploy it. Pick your hub's engine and press **Deploy**.

The first start downloads the app, which takes a minute or two. When the **Running** count in the
app's header shows 1, it is ready.

</Step>
<Step>

## Run it on an image

First you need an image. Basiks can make one:

1. Open **Actions** in the sidebar and search for **Generate Test Image**.
2. Press its play button, choose `human_mitosis` (a microscopy image of nuclei) and press **Do**.
3. The new image pops up in the bottom right corner when it is ready. Click it to open it in the
   viewer.

Now apply an action to it:

4. Right-click the image and choose **Otsu Thresholding** from the actions offered for it.
5. Leave the options as they are and press **Do**.
6. The result, a mask of the nuclei, pops up when it is done. Click it to view it.

That's the whole loop: an app announced what it can do, you picked an action, the hub sent the
work to the app, and the result was stored next to the image it came from. Arkitekt also recorded
that the mask was derived from that image, by a task you started. That record of where every
result came from is [provenance](/docs/about/provenance).

</Step>

<Callout type="info" title="Running a hub for a lab or a facility?">

This quickstart puts a hub on your own computer, reachable from your network. A hub that a whole
lab relies on needs a bit more thought: where the data lives, how people reach it, and who
administers it. Start with [What you are setting up](/docs/run/what-you-are-setting-up) and
[Planning](/docs/run/planning).

</Callout>

<Callout type="info" title="Something did not work?">

- **The command says Docker is missing or not running.** Start Docker Desktop (or the Docker
  service on Linux) and run the command again.
- **The hub is not in the Assign to hub list.** The hub was not accepted in step 1, or is still
  starting. `konstruktor list` shows the hubs on this machine, and `konstruktor status my-hub` what
  is running.
- **Generate Test Image is not in the Actions list.** The app is not running yet. Open the App
  Store, find Basiks and check that it shows as running.

More in [Troubleshooting](/docs/use/troubleshooting).

</Callout>
